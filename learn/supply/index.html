Go deeper · Supply and emission

Where every coin comes from

There will only ever be about 28.7 billion . Most already exist. The rest trickle out to miners on a fixed schedule that ends around 2057, and nobody can change it without every node agreeing.

Why it matters to you

If you hold a coin, you want to know how many more will be made. More coins mean each one is a smaller share. With Kaspa the answer is fixed and public: no company can print more, and there was no early sale to insiders.

How it works: a smooth step down

Every new block pays its miner in freshly made coins. Bitcoin cuts that pay in half every four years in one jump, a . Kaspa cuts it the same amount over a year, in twelve monthly steps of about 5.6%, so there is never a cliff.

What isn’t settled

Nothing about the schedule. The open question is what happens once new coins are tiny, and miners have to live on s instead. That has its own page.
